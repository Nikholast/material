Opening: ask the class two questions. 'What will you do at 8 p.m. tonight?' and 'Have you finished your homework?' Do not correct them yet. Tell them that by the end of this unit they will be able to answer both correctly.

What we learned last time

Three tenses you already know. We build on these today.

Present Continuous

am / is / are + V-ing

I am studying now.

Happening right now

Past Continuous

was / were + V-ing

I was studying at 8 p.m.

Was happening in the past

Simple Future

will + V1

I will study tonight.

Will happen later

Notice: all three use V-ing or V1. Today we add V-ing in the future, and a new verb form: V3.
Keep this to four minutes. Point at random students and ask them to make one sentence in each tense. This is a warm-up, not new teaching.

Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

1

Use the Future Continuous

Say what will be happening at a specific time in the future.

2

Use the Present Perfect

Talk about experience and about past actions that matter now.

3

Use the Past Perfect

Show which of two past events happened first.

4

Use the Future Perfect

Say that something will be finished before a future deadline.

Read each objective aloud. Ask one student to repeat objective 3 in their own words. Tell them objective 2 is the hardest and we will spend the most time on it.

Today's road map

Four new tenses, and where they sit on the timeline. Tap a tense to zoom in.

The key idea for this whole unit. Continuous tenses ask: "What is / was / will be in progress?" Perfect tenses ask: "Is it already finished by that moment?"
Draw this timeline on the whiteboard too. Students remember a timeline they watched being drawn much better than one that simply appears on a screen. Repeat the two key questions at the bottom twice.
Part 1

Future Continuous

will be + V-ing something in progress at a future moment

1
Start here because it is the easiest of the four. Students already know V-ing from present and past continuous. Only the auxiliary changes.

Future Continuous: the big idea

An action that will be in progress at a certain time in the future.

At 8 p.m. tomorrow, I will be studying English.

Simple Future

"I will study tomorrow."

Only says the action will happen. No exact moment.

Future Continuous

"I will be studying at 8 p.m."

The action is already in progress at that moment.

Physical demonstration: start writing on the board, then stop and say 'At this exact second I am writing.' Then point to the clock: 'At 8 p.m. tomorrow I will be writing.' The words ALREADY STARTED are the key idea.

Future Continuous: the formula

One pattern for every subject. Nothing changes.

Subject+will be+V-ing
No he/she/it rule here. I, you, he, she, we, they all use will be.
I will be working at 9 a.m. tomorrow.
She will be sleeping when you arrive.
They will be waiting for us at the station.
Have students copy the three sentences using two pen colours: red for WILL BE, purple for the V-ing. The screen already uses the same colours, so they can copy exactly what they see.

When do we use it?

Three common situations.

1

In progress at an exact future time

At 10 o'clock tomorrow, I will be taking the test.

2

Interrupted by another future action

She will be cooking when her father comes home.

3

A polite question about plans

Will you be using the computer this afternoon?

Use 3 is very useful at work. It sounds more polite than "Will you use the computer?"
Use 2 links back to past continuous (was cooking when he came home). Point that connection out clearly. Use 3 is worth practising because students will need it in industrial placement.

Time signals for Future Continuous

These words tell you the Future Continuous is the right choice.

at + exact time

at 8 p.m. tomorrow
at 10 o'clock tonight

this time + future

this time tomorrow
at this time next week
this time next month

when / while + clause

when you arrive
while you are sleeping

Careful with when and while. After when / while, do not use will. Use the simple present.
I will be sleeping when you arrive.    I will be sleeping when you will arrive.
The 'no will after when' rule is the single most common mistake with this tense. Write both sentences on the board, one with a tick and one with a cross, and leave them there for the rest of the lesson.

Negative and question forms

Future Continuous: the pattern barely changes.

Negative

S + will not (won't) be + V-ing

I won't be working tomorrow.
She won't be joining us tonight.
They won't be waiting for you.

Question

Will + S + be + V-ing ?

Will you be using the lab?
Yes, I will. / No, I won't.
What will you be doing at 7 p.m.?
I will be having dinner.

Pair drill for five minutes: student A asks 'What will you be doing at ___?' and student B answers in a full sentence. Write six times on the board for them to choose from.

Exercise 1: Future Continuous

Complete each sentence with will be + V-ing. Then swap books with your partner and check.

Answer key: 1 will be working, 2 will be having, 3 will be travelling, 4 Will ... be using, 5 will not be waiting / won't be waiting, 6 will be repairing, 7 will ... be doing, 8 will not be attending / won't be attending. Walk around and watch for students writing 'will be work' without -ing.
Part 2

Before the perfect tenses: V3

All three perfect tenses need the third form of the verb

2
Be honest with the class: if their V3 is weak, every perfect tense will fail. Spend real time here. This is the foundation slide set.

V1, V2 and V3

Every English verb has three main forms.

V1

Base form

eat, go, write, work

Used in: Simple Present, Future

V2

Past form

ate, went, wrote, worked

Used in: Simple Past

V3

Past participle

eaten, gone, written, worked

Used in: all perfect tenses (today)

Write eat - ate - eaten on the board. Have the whole class say it aloud three times. Then do go - went - gone. Rhythm and repetition matter more than explanation here.

Regular verbs: V2 and V3 are the same

Good news: for regular verbs you only add -ed, and V2 = V3.

V1+-ed=V2 and V3

Tap 'Tambah kata bengkel' to add workshop verbs: weld, drill, assemble, tighten. Making it their subject makes it stick. Ask students for more verbs from their own jurusan.

Exercise 2: write the V3 form

Warm-up before we start the perfect tenses. Two minutes only.

Answer key: 1 gone, 2 eaten, 3 written, 4 finished, 5 seen, 6 studied, 7 drunk, 8 taken. Do this orally as well as in writing so you can hear the pronunciation.
Part 3

Present Perfect

have / has + V3 the past that still matters now

3
Bridge for Indonesian speakers: we have the words 'sudah' and 'pernah'. English has no single word for these, so it uses have/has + V3 instead. Give this bridge in English but let students make the connection themselves.

Present Perfect: the big idea

The action happened in the past, but the result is important now.

Present Perfect

I have lost my key.

I lost it earlier, and right now I still cannot open my door. The result matters.

Simple Past

I lost my key yesterday.

This only tells us when it happened. It says nothing about now.

Best demonstration in the unit: take your key out, hide it, then say 'I have lost my key' and mime being locked out. Students see the present result. Do not rush this slide.

Present Perfect: the formula

Only one part changes: have or has.

Subject+have / has+V3

have

I, you, we, they, plural subjects

They have arrived.

has

he, she, it, singular subjects

Rina has arrived.

Quick check: guess first, then tap each subject.

Trick to give them: if the subject can be replaced by HE, SHE or IT, use HAS. Everything else takes HAVE. Same rule they already know from simple present (he goes, she works). Point at a subject, let the class shout HAVE or HAS, then tap to check.

Three uses of the Present Perfect

Different situations, but always the same formula.

1

Life experience

Have you ever eaten sushi?
I have never been to Jakarta.

Signals: ever, never, before

2

Finished action, result now

She has just left the room.
I haven't finished my work yet.

Signals: just, already, yet

3

From the past until now

I have studied here since 2019.
He has worked here for ten years.

Signals: since, for

Use 1 is the easiest and the most fun. Start there. Use 3 is where students make the since/for mistake, so slow down on it.

Since, for, and the small words

Get these right and most of your sentences will be correct.

since + a starting point

since 2019
since Monday
since I was a child

Answers: when did it start?

for + a length of time

for three years
for two hours
for a long time

Answers: how long?

already

I have already eaten.

between have/has and V3

just

She has just left.

between have/has and V3

yet

I haven't eaten yet.

at the end of the sentence

Since or for?1 / 10
Pilih jawaban yang benar.
Fast drill: read the sentence on the game card, class shouts SINCE or FOR, then tap the answer. Ten rounds. The most common error is 'since three years'.

Negative and question forms

Present Perfect: add not, or move have/has to the front.

Negative

S + have not / has not + V3

have not = haven't   has not = hasn't

I haven't finished my work.
She hasn't arrived yet.

Question

Have / Has + S + V3 ?

Have you finished?
Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
Has she called you?
Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.

Insist on the short answer form: 'Yes, I have.' NOT 'Yes, I have finished.' Students who repeat the verb are copying Indonesian sentence habits.

The hardest part of this unit. The time expression decides.

Simple rule to remember: if you see yesterday, last, or ago, use the Simple Past. Always.
Write the simple rule at the bottom in large letters on the board. It will get students through about 80% of exam questions on this contrast.

Exercise 3: Present Perfect

Complete with have / has + V3. Numbers 3 and 7 are questions, so be careful with the word order.

Answer key: 1 have finished, 2 hasn't eaten, 3 Have ... visited, 4 have lived, 5 has worked, 6 haven't seen, 7 Has ... arrived, 8 has just repaired.

Exercise 4: Present Perfect or Simple Past?

Look at the time expression first. Compare each pair (1 and 2, 3 and 4...): the difference is always the time expression.

Answer key: 1 saw, 2 have seen, 3 went, 4 has never been / has never gone, 5 finished, 6 have already finished, 7 Have ... done, 8 did ... do. Discuss the pairs side by side; the contrast is the whole point of this exercise.
Part 4

Past Perfect

had + V3 the past before the past

4
Good news for the class: if they understand present perfect, past perfect is much easier. Only the auxiliary changes, and there is no singular/plural rule at all.

Past Perfect: the big idea

Two past events, and you want to show which one came first.

When I arrived at school, the bell had already rung.

The bell rang first (had rung). Then I arrived (arrived). Both are in the past, but one came earlier. Think of it as a flashback in a film: the older event gets had + V3.
Draw the timeline on the board with the students. Ask them which happened first before you reveal the answer. The flashback analogy works well with this age group.

Past Perfect: formula and linking words

Had is used for every subject. No exceptions.

Subject+had+V3

before

I had finished my homework before my father came home.

after

After she had eaten, she went to bed.

when

When we arrived, the movie had already started.

by the time

By the time the guests arrived, we had cleaned the house.

Practice activity: give two separate sentences (She ate. She went to bed.) and ask students to join them using 'after' plus the past perfect. Do five pairs.

Negative, question, and the 3-second test

Same pattern as the present perfect. Just change have/has to had.

Negative

S + had not (hadn't) + V3

I hadn't finished my work when the bell rang.

Question

Had + S + V3 ?

Had you finished the work?
Yes, I had. / No, I hadn't.

Decide in three seconds: are there two past events in this sentence?
If no, just use the Simple Past (V2): "I ate nasi goreng last night."
If yes, the earlier event takes had + V3, the later one takes V2.
Make students write the three-second test on the inside cover of their notebook. Then test them orally with five mixed sentences.

Exercise 5: Past Perfect

Use had + V3, or the Simple Past where the past perfect is not needed. Careful with number 7.

Answer key: 1 had already started, 2 had finished, 3 had eaten, 4 had left, 5 hadn't met, 6 Had ... finished, 7 cleaned (simple past, only one event), 8 had gone. Number 7 is the trap; discuss it with the whole class.
Part 5

Future Perfect

will have + V3 finished before a future deadline

5
Last tense of the unit. Tell students it is the easiest to build because they now know all the pieces: will, have, and V3.

Future Perfect: the big idea

Something will already be finished before a certain time in the future.

By 5 p.m., I will have finished the report.

Look at the future moment first, then look back. Is the action already complete at that moment? If yes, use the Future Perfect.
Concrete example from their world: 'By the time you graduate, you will have completed your industrial placement.' Personal and future-focused examples motivate this age group.

Future Perfect: the formula

Three words, always in the same order, for every subject.

Subject+will have+V3
Never "will has". It is always will have, for I, you, he, she, we and they.
I will have finished the report by 5 p.m.
She will have graduated by next June.
They will have built the house before the rainy season.

Common time signals: by, by the time, by then, before, in two years' time.

Point out that 'by' is the key preposition here. 'By 5 p.m.' means at or before 5 p.m. Contrast with 'at 5 p.m.' which would need the future continuous.

Future Perfect vs Future Continuous

Both talk about the future, but they answer different questions.

Future Continuous: at 5 p.m.

"At 5 p.m. I will be writing the report."

Still in progress at 5 p.m. Not finished.

Future Perfect: by 5 p.m.

"By 5 p.m. I will have written the report."

Completely finished before 5 p.m.

Negative: I won't have finished by then.

Question: Will you have finished by 5 p.m.?

The AT vs BY contrast is the clearest way to teach this. AT a time = future continuous. BY a time = future perfect. Write those two lines on the board.

Exercise 6: Future Perfect

Complete with will have + V3. Every sentence contains by or by the time: that is your signal.

Answer key: 1 will have finished, 2 will have graduated, 3 Will ... have completed, 4 will have already eaten, 5 won't have repaired, 6 will have learned/learnt, 7 will have produced, 8 Will ... have packed.

Common mistakes to avoid

These four errors appear in almost every class test. Find the mistake, then tap to check.

Let students find the errors themselves before you tap each card. Discovering the mistake is far more memorable than being told.

Exercise 7: Mixed practice

Which moment in time am I looking at, and is the action finished? After checking, the tense name appears next to each answer.

Answer key: 1 will be working (FC), 2 have never seen (PresP), 3 had left (PastP), 4 will have saved (FutP), 5 Have ... finished (PresP), 6 will be having (FC), 7 had worked (PastP), 8 have lived (PresP). Ask students to write the tense name next to each answer.

Exercise 8: build your own sentences

Write one true sentence about yourself for each prompt. Then read two of them aloud to your partner.

This is the most valuable exercise in the unit because the sentences are personal and therefore memorable. Type a volunteer's sentence and press 'Cek pola' to check the formula live with the class.
